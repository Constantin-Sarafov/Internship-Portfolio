Overview

THE
GAME.

This game jam project is a collection of 3D minigames where everyday tasks are turned into short, high pressure challenges such as a Frogger like computer game, a timed sandwich making task, a TV remote input challenge, and a precision card swipe mechanic.

The experience is comedic and intentionally stressful, focusing on lighthearted frustration rather than a traditional win or lose structure. Players move through a sequence of minigames and receive a final score based on performance, creating a short replayable “bad day simulator.”

The Theme

RESPONDING
TO THE BRIEF.

The jam theme was “ragebait,” which I interpreted as designing interactions that intentionally create frustration through simple but unpredictable gameplay moments. Instead of taking it literally, I focused on turning familiar tasks into slightly unfair or awkward challenges that create tension and comedic frustration.

Early on, we considered building a single continuous experience around one mechanic, but this quickly felt too limited for the variety we wanted. We pivoted to a structure of multiple short minigames, which allowed each idea to explore a different type of frustration such as timing, spatial awareness, and precision under pressure.

48hr Process

HOUR BY
HOUR.

This jam was built under tight time pressure, so development was heavily focused on quickly prototyping ideas, identifying what worked, and cutting anything that didn’t fit the core experience.

H0
Theme drop — ideation We brainstormed ideas around “ragebait,” focusing on turning simple interactions into short frustrating minigames that could be understood instantly under pressure.

H8
Core loop prototype A playable build connected multiple minigames through a simple flow system, confirming that variety and rapid switching created stronger tension than a single mechanic.

H16
Halfway — what got cut We cut a popup “computer virus” minigame due to time constraints and prioritised polishing the existing minigames for stability and consistency.

H32
Submission The final build is a sequence of short 3D minigames built for fast paced comedic frustration. I’m most satisfied with the variety achieved within the time limit.

Lessons Learned

WHAT I
TOOK AWAY.

This being my first game jam, the 32 hour timeframe made it clear how much design depends on scope, speed, and decision making under pressure, where ideas constantly need to be tested, adjusted, or cut.

01
Designing under extreme time pressure This being my first ever game jam, I learned how quickly design decisions need to be made under extreme time pressure. It revealed that I naturally focus on building small, testable interactions first rather than overplanning systems.
02
Scope control is a core design skill If I had the same constraints again, I would commit earlier to cutting ideas instead of trying to keep too many concepts alive at once, as scope control became the main challenge throughout development.
03
Turning chaos into a finished product I’m most proud of how we managed to turn a large set of ideas into a cohesive playable experience within 48 hours, especially given that this was my first game jam and my first time working under this level of time pressure.
